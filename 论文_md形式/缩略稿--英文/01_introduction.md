## I. Introduction

User review analysis is a foundational task in intelligent e-commerce operations. Traditional methods usually focus on sentiment classification, topic clustering, or aspect-based sentiment analysis. These methods can summarize users' positive or negative attitudes toward product attributes, but they less often answer whether those review opinions can be explained, supported, or contradicted by evidence from the product itself. For product operations and product improvement, it is not enough to know only that "users complain about limited capacity." Analysts also need to know whether the product page explicitly describes the dimensions, whether the images show the spatial structure, and whether the issue reflects a product defect or a gap in user expectations.

Large language models create new possibilities for product review summarization, but one-step summarization carries clear risks. If a system directly feeds product copy, image descriptions, and reviews into a model as a whole, the model may generate fluent conclusions that are difficult to audit. Users may struggle to determine which reviews, product texts, or images support a given recommendation, and they may also find it difficult to locate whether an error occurred during review understanding, retrieval, evidence interpretation, or final generation. Therefore, product VOC analysis should not be reduced to a black-box summarization task. It should instead be modeled as a traceable chain from review needs to product evidence and then to a structured report.

The research question of this paper is: how can subjective experiences in user reviews be explicitly aligned with verifiable evidence in product copy and product images in an e-commerce product setting, so as to generate VOC insights that are auditable, reproducible, and evaluable? To address this question, this paper proposes Decathlon VOC Analyzer. The system constructs standardized evidence packages centered on products, extracts reviews into aspect-level objects, uses a question-planning layer to convert each aspect into a text, image, or joint image-text retrieval task, and finally generates structured reports through multimodal recall, reranking, evidence aggregation, and claim-level attribution.
